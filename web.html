What Is BMI (Body Mass Index)?

Body Mass Index, or BMI, is a simple number that compares your weight to your height. Doctors, dietitians, and public health organizations use it as a quick screening tool to see whether an adult may be underweight, at a healthy weight, overweight, or living with obesity. Because it needs only two measurements, anyone can calculate it at home without special equipment.

How to Calculate BMI

The BMI formula is the same everywhere; only the units change. With metric units, divide your weight in kilograms by your height in meters squared. For example, a person who weighs 70 kg and is 1.75 m tall has a BMI of 70 ÷ (1.75 × 1.75), which is about 22.9.

With imperial units, multiply your weight in pounds by 703, then divide by your height in inches squared. Our BMI calculator above does this math for you instantly, so you can simply enter your numbers and read the result.

BMI Categories for Adults

The World Health Organization groups adult BMI results into the following ranges:

Adult BMI categories (WHO)
BMICategory
Below 18.5Underweight
18.5 – 24.9Healthy weight
25.0 – 29.9Overweight
30.0 and aboveObesity

These ranges apply to adults aged 20 and older. Children and teenagers are assessed differently, using BMI-for-age percentiles that account for growth and gender.

Is BMI Accurate? Understanding the Limits

BMI is useful, but it is not a diagnosis. It cannot tell the difference between muscle and fat, so a muscular athlete may be labeled overweight despite low body fat. It also ignores where fat is stored, and fat around the waist carries more health risk than fat elsewhere. Age, sex, and ethnicity can change how BMI relates to health, and some health bodies suggest lower cut-offs for people of Asian descent.

For a fuller picture, combine BMI with waist circumference, blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol, and your family history. A healthcare professional can interpret these together.

Simple Tips for Reaching a Healthy Weight

  • Eat balanced meals: Focus on vegetables, fruit, whole grains, lean protein, and healthy fats.
  • Move regularly: Adults should aim for at least 150 minutes of moderate activity each week.
  • Sleep well: Poor sleep can disrupt hunger hormones and make weight control harder.
  • Drink water: Choose water over sugary drinks to cut unnecessary calories.
  • Be consistent: Small, steady habits beat crash diets and last longer.

Frequently Asked Questions About BMI

What is a healthy BMI range?

For most adults, a BMI from 18.5 to 24.9 is considered healthy.

Is BMI accurate for athletes?

Not always. Because BMI ignores muscle mass, athletes and very muscular people can receive a misleading result.

How often should I check my BMI?

Every few months is plenty, since meaningful changes in weight happen gradually.
